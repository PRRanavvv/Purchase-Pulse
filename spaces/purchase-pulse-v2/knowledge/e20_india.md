# E20 India Knowledge Notes

## What E20 changes for buyers

E20 is petrol blended with 20 percent ethanol. For a used-car buyer, the practical concern is not only engine damage. The buyer also needs to think about mileage impact, vehicle generation, fuel-system materials, OEM guidance, service history, and whether the exact model was designed or tuned for higher ethanol blends.

## Conservative generation rules

Use model year as a first-pass signal, then verify with the vehicle manual or authorised service center.

- Pre-2023 petrol vehicles: treat as older or uncertain E20-readiness unless the OEM says otherwise.
- 2023 to 2024 petrol vehicles: treat as transition vehicles. They may be more material-compliant, but do not assume full E20 tuning.
- 2025 onward petrol vehicles: treat as likely E20-tuned, with lower buyer concern.

These are broad product rules, not a mechanical diagnosis.

## Mileage impact assumptions

Use mileage loss as an ownership-cost assumption, not as a scare claim.

- Older petrol vehicles can have a larger mileage penalty under E20.
- Transition vehicles should be checked model by model.
- Newer E20-tuned vehicles should have lower mileage impact.

The app should present this as a range and mark confidence clearly.

## Purchase checklist for E20

Ask for:

- owner manual or OEM fuel guidance for the exact model and variant
- service records for fuel filter, injector cleaning, fuel lines, and engine tune-ups
- recent real-world mileage before and after the local E20 rollout, if the owner has it
- authorised-service-center inspection notes for older petrol cars

## Product wording

Avoid saying "E20 will damage this car" unless there is model-specific evidence. Better wording:

- E20 confidence
- expected running-cost impact
- compatibility verification needed
- buyer-risk level
